Getting started

        
    
          
  1. Plug the micro:bit into the computer with a USB cable.
    2. 
          
  2. First time only: click First-time setup and put MicroPython on it.
    3. 
          
  3. Click Connect micro:bit and pick it from the list.
    4. 
          
  4. Drag blocks and click Run. The result shows up on the LEDs.
    5. 
          
  5. Save to micro:bit keeps your program, so it runs every time it turns on, even on batteries.
    6. 
        

        
The LEDs

        
0,04,4

          
There are 5 × 5 LEDs. x goes from 0 on the left to 4 on the right, and y from 0 at the top to 4 at the bottom. Brightness goes from 0 (off) to 9.

        
Tips

        
    
          
  • Blocks marked V2 in their tooltip (logo, sound level, sounds, volume) need a micro:bit V2, the one with a gold logo and a speaker.
    • 
          
  • The compass asks you to tilt the board and fill the LEDs the first time. That's normal.
    • 
          
  • Radio: micro:bits only hear each other when they use the same group number.
    • 
          
  • The Colour screen blocks are for the Waveshare 1.8 inch LCD for micro:bit (160 × 128). x goes from 0 to 159 and y from 0 to 127. The screen uses pins P1 and P2, so don't use those in the Pins blocks at the same time. It needs a micro:bit V2.
    • 
          
  • Pins P0, P1 and P2 are the big gold rings. They work with 3 V only. Always put a resistor in front of an LED.
    • 
          
  • If the micro:bit stops responding: click Stop. If that doesn't help, press the reset button on the back and connect again.
    •

Put MicroPython on the micro:bit (first time only)

    
You do this once for each micro:bit. It replaces whatever program was on it.

    
    
      
  1. Plug the micro:bit into the computer.
    2. 
      
  2. Open python.microbit.org in Chrome or Edge.
    3. 
      
  3. Click Send to micro:bit and follow the steps. This puts MicroPython on it.
    4. 
      
  4. Close that tab, come back here and click Connect micro:bit.
    5. 
    

    
Already used python.microbit.org with this micro:bit? Then it's ready, and you can skip this.
